Privacy

What happens to your data on this site, stated precisely.

Last updated 2026-09-13. This page describes the site as it is built. If a sentence here stops matching the code, the sentence is the bug — say so and it gets fixed.

Nothing you type is transmitted

The calculator, the collapse list, the split planner and the containment check are arithmetic on integers. They run in the page you already downloaded: there is no upload endpoint, no backend, no account system and no database behind this site.

The engine ships with the page. Open `view-source:https://cidrkit.com/assets/cidr.js` and search for `fetch`, `XMLHttpRequest` or `sendBeacon` — none of those identifiers appear anywhere in it. That is a check you can actually run, not a promise.

No registry lookups, on purpose

A subnet calculator can be made to look clever by asking IANA who owns a block. This one does not: it reports the categories from RFC 6890 as compiled into the code, and it will tell you when a range is unallocated-looking rather than pretend to know. Asking a registry would mean your network plan left your browser, and a network plan is exactly the sort of thing worth keeping local.

The same reasoning is why there is no `whois` button here.

Who does see something

These files are delivered by GitHub Pages from an IP in `185.199.108.0/22`. Like every web server, GitHub's edge records the standard request line: your IP address, user agent string, the path requested, a timestamp and the `Referer` header if your browser sent one. That log exists whether or not you type anything, because requesting the page is what makes the page appear.

If you reached this page from a search engine, the search engine also knows you asked. That is on their side, not ours.

Ads and consent

This site is served with Google AdSense enabled. Adverts come from `pagead2.googlesyndication.com` and `googleadservices.com`, so your browser talks to Google when you load a page here — that is a third-party request, and it is visible in a network panel. We reworded every sentence that would stop being true, rather than keep it.

Google uses cookies to serve ads based on your prior visits to this or other sites. You can opt out at https://www.google.com/settings/ads or at https://www.aboutads.info/choices; how Google uses data it collects through its ad platform is described at https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland you should see a consent message before any personalised advertising. The amount of data shared with Google follows that consent choice, not anything on this site.

Advertising revenue does not change what the calculator does: the arithmetic path is identical with an advert on the page or without one.

Analytics, cookies and storage

This build ships no analytics of any kind; if one is ever added, the request shows up in your own network panel and this paragraph gets rewritten. Nothing measures which prefixes you typed or how long you stayed — a counter would need a request to whoever serves it, and this site makes none for that purpose.

The site sets no cookies of its own and keeps nothing in `localStorage` or `IndexedDB`. Reload the page and your input is gone. With advertising enabled Google may set cookies on its own domains; those are theirs to read, not ours.

How the numbers are checked

Every field the calculator reports is compared against Python's standard-library `ipaddress` module, on 356 generated prefixes covering both address families and the boundary cases, and on 15 malformed inputs that both implementations must reject. The vectors and the comparison run in the browser, on this origin: /test/ shows the result of each assertion. That page is not indexed and is not a promise — it is the test, executing.

Where the two disagree, the bug is in this site and gets fixed here rather than explained away.

Contact

A wrong number, or a sentence on this page that no longer matches reality: open an issue on https://github.com/george-poe/cidrkit-com. The pages and their tests are built in that repository, so a report lands next to the code that would have to change. There is no email list.

Every calculation runs inside your browser tab. Nothing you paste is sent to a server, because there is no server call in the code path.
